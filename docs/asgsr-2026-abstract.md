# When the Spleen Stops Building: Stromal Remodelling and the Loss of Blood and Immune Cell Production in Spaceflight

The Spleen Machine: Kireeti, Pranav, Aryan and Vedant. NASA GeneLab for High Schools 2026.

Accepted for the High School/Middle School Student Poster Session, ASGSR 2026 Annual Meeting, Crystal City, VA, December 5, 2026.

## Abstract

Spleen tissue from Rodent Research-9 (OSD-420/GLDS-420) was collected as a secondary biospecimen from mice studied for ocular, skeletal, and fluid-shift changes under microgravity, leaving its transcriptional response to spaceflight unexamined. We reanalyzed the raw OSD-420 RNA-seq counts (20 male C57BL/6J mice, 10 flight and 10 ground control, 33-day ISS mission) with a corrected DESeq2 pipeline that removed ERCC spike-ins and dissection-carryover liver and pancreatic transcripts before independent filtering. This yielded 146 differentially expressed genes (83 up, 63 down) at adjusted p<0.05 across 10,546 testable genes, superseding a prior ten-gene result from this dataset that we show was a contamination artifact of an uncontrolled variance filter. Gene-set enrichment and rank-sum testing converge on three lines of evidence: upregulated stromal and perivascular remodeling genes (Gpx3, Sparcl1, Pdgfra, TGF-β/BMP signaling), downregulated erythroid and heme biosynthesis transcripts, and suppressed type I interferon-stimulated genes. Because Hurricane Irma delayed ground-control dissection by nine months, cohort identity, dissection date, and library batch are fully collinear with spaceflight status, an unresolvable limitation. We propose that spaceflight remodels the splenic stromal niche, and that this remodeling, rather than direct leukocyte injury, drives the loss of erythropoietic output, germinal-center organization, and interferon tone underlying post-flight immune decline. Three independent aims test this model using a ground-based hindlimb-unloading spaceflight analog. Aim 1 asks whether the erythroid decline reflects loss of splenic erythropoiesis rather than residual blood, using perfused spleens, Ter119/CD71 flow cytometry, and CFU-E colony assays. Aim 2 tests whether stromal expansion disorganizes germinal-center architecture, using single-nucleus RNA-seq, immunofluorescence, and NP-KLH immunization with germinal-center scoring. Aim 3 determines whether the reduced interferon signature lowers antiviral competence, using ex vivo poly(I:C)/IFN-β stimulation and in vivo viral challenge. If the model holds, the splenic stroma, not the leukocyte compartment, becomes the target for countermeasures protecting astronaut immune and hematologic function on long-duration missions.
